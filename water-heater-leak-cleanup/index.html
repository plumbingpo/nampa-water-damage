Water Heater Leak Cleanup Help in Nampa

A water heater holds 40 to 80 gallons, but that is not the limit of the leak. Your supply line keeps feeding the tank, which means it fills back up just as quickly as water escapes through a rusted bottom. Nothing stops the flow except shutting the cold water valve. Where the unit sits decides the damage: a garage floor, a hallway closet or, worst of all, an attic above finished rooms.

After extracting the water, our Nampa crew checks the materials that sat next to the unit. The platform beneath the tank and the base of the closet walls are usually soaked. In a garage, water reaches the wall shared with the house and anything stored on the floor. From an attic it comes down through insulation and ceilings. We measure each area and dry it with air movers and dehumidifiers.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Cut the Power or GasIf the heater is electric, turn its breaker off. For gas, turn the control knob to off. A burner or heating element must not stay on once the tank is empty.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveThe cold line over the tank has a valve, and it closes with a clockwise turn. When that valve leaks or will not move, close the main water valve to the house.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterWater from the tank can scald. Pets and children should stay clear, and nobody should walk into pooled water before it cools down.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideWhen the drain valve is within reach, connect a garden hose and lead it to a floor drain or outdoors, which keeps the rest of the water off the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorLift boxes, rugs and anything absorbent off the wet floor. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.
Our Process

Our Water Heater Leak Cleanup Process

1

Remove Standing Water

We remove standing water from the floor, closet or garage and, for attic units, from the overflow pan and ceiling cavity, so the surrounding materials stop absorbing it.

2

Check Around the Heater

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check the platform, wall bases, adjoining rooms and any ceiling below to find the full extent of the wet area.

3

Remove Wet Material

Soaked insulation, crumbling drywall and damaged baseboards are taken out. For an attic leak, the wet insulation is cleared out, which lets us dry the drywall and ceiling joists beneath.

4

Dry and Monitor

We keep the drying equipment going for the usual 3 to 5 days needed to dry the space. We record moisture readings and tell you when the space is ready for the new heater.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • Rust at the connections above the tank, either the flex lines or the inlet and outlet fittings.
  • A drain valve that loosens, cracks or no longer closes all the way.
  • Sediment and corrosion that eat through the bottom of an aging steel tank.
  • Pressure rising too high in the tank because the thermostat failed or no expansion tank was installed.
  • Anode rods that are used up, leaving the steel inside the tank open to rust.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Garage Wall Base

As water runs across the garage slab, it wets the drywall and bottom plate of the wall the garage shares with the house and continues into flooring on the other side.

Heater Closet Drywall

Drywall surrounding a heater closet inside the house wicks water up and lets it through to the neighboring rooms. Baseboards swell and the lower wall stays wet inside.

Attic and Ceilings

When an attic heater leaks, water passes through the insulation and lands on the ceiling underneath. Soaked insulation loses its insulating value and gets heavy enough to collapse the drywall ceiling.

Stand and Subfloor Damage

More water goes into the subfloor or wooden stand beneath the tank than anywhere else. That wood may rot and lose strength, and it will have to carry the weight of a new tank filled with water.

Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

A sudden tank failure is often treated as accidental and sudden, so the resulting damage to belongings, floors and walls is often covered. Usually the unit is left out of the claim, as age related wear is viewed as a maintenance matter. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Check your policy, photograph the heater and the damage, and keep the old unit until the claim is settled.
